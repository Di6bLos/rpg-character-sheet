A character is built from an ancestry, a background and a class, which together set six attribute modifiers, a set of proficiencies and starting Hit Points.

## Attribute modifiers

There are six: **Strength**, **Dexterity** and **Constitution** (physical), **Intelligence**, **Wisdom** and **Charisma** (mental). Each starts at +0.

| Attribute    | What it adds to                                                                |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Strength     | Melee damage, how much you can carry, Athletics                                |
| Dexterity    | AC, Reflex saves, ranged attacks, Acrobatics, Stealth, Thievery                |
| Constitution | Hit Points and Fortitude saves                                                 |
| Intelligence | Extra trained skills and languages; Arcana, Crafting, Lore, Occultism, Society |
| Wisdom       | Perception and Will saves; Medicine, Nature, Religion, Survival                |
| Charisma     | Deception, Diplomacy, Intimidation, Performance                                |

- An **attribute boost** raises a modifier by 1. Boosts you gain at the same time must go to different attributes.
- If the modifier is already **+4 or higher**, a boost only marks a **partial boost**; the next boost to it turns the partial boost into +1.
- An **attribute flaw** lowers a modifier by 1.
- At 1st level no modifier can be higher than +4.

_Player Core pp. 17, 400._

## Building a 1st-level character

1. **Ancestry**: its boosts and flaws (or two free boosts instead), its Hit Points, size, Speed and languages, a heritage and an ancestry feat.
2. **Background**: two boosts (one from a choice of two, one free), training in a skill and a Lore skill, and a skill feat.
3. **Class**: a boost to its key attribute, its Hit Points, initial proficiencies and 1st-level features.
4. **Four free boosts**, each to a different attribute. You should end up between –1 and +4 everywhere.
5. **Hit Points** = ancestry HP + class HP (the class number plus your Constitution modifier).
6. **Equipment**: 15 gp (150 sp) to spend.

_Player Core pp. 19–25._

## Perception, saves and skills

- **Perception** = Wisdom + proficiency. It's what you usually roll for initiative.
- **Saving throws**: **Fortitude** (Constitution), **Reflex** (Dexterity) and **Will** (Wisdom), each + proficiency. You're always at least trained in all three.
- **Skill modifier** = the skill's attribute + proficiency + other bonuses and penalties.

| Skill        | Attribute | Skill       | Attribute |
| ------------ | --------- | ----------- | --------- |
| Acrobatics   | Dex       | Nature      | Wis       |
| Arcana       | Int       | Occultism   | Int       |
| Athletics    | Str       | Performance | Cha       |
| Crafting     | Int       | Religion    | Wis       |
| Deception    | Cha       | Society     | Int       |
| Diplomacy    | Cha       | Stealth     | Dex       |
| Intimidation | Cha       | Survival    | Wis       |
| Lore         | Int       | Thievery    | Dex       |
| Medicine     | Wis       |             |           |

- Anyone can use a skill's **untrained actions**; its **trained actions** need trained proficiency or better.
- If two sources would make you trained in the same skill, pick another skill instead (a Lore for a Lore).
- Armor with a check penalty applies it to Strength- and Dexterity-based skill checks, except those with the attack trait.

_Player Core pp. 25–26, 225–226, 404–405._

## Armor Class and class DC

- **AC** = 10 + Dexterity (up to your armor's Dex cap) + proficiency for your armor category + the armor's item bonus + other bonuses and penalties. Without armor, use your unarmored defense proficiency.
- **Class DC** = 10 + your class DC proficiency + your key attribute. Many class abilities use it.
- **Spell DC** = 10 + your spellcasting attribute + proficiency; your **spell attack** is the same without the 10.

_Player Core pp. 27, 403–404._

## Feats

- **Ancestry feats** come from your ancestry, **class feats** from your class.
- **Skill feats** need training in their skill; **general feats** are open to anyone who meets the prerequisites (you can take a skill feat in a general feat slot).
- Class feats can also be spent on an **archetype**'s dedication and feats.

_Player Core pp. 92, 215._
