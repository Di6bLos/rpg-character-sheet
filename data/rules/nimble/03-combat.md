Combat in Nimble is fast: heroes act first, every attack is a single roll of its damage dice, and a 1 on the Primary Die misses.

## Rolling initiative

The GM says "Roll Initiative!" and each hero rolls **d20 + Initiative** (usually DEX). The result sets how many actions you have on your first turn:

| Initiative            | Actions on your first turn |
| --------------------- | -------------------------- |
| 9 or less             | 1                          |
| 10–19                 | 2                          |
| 20+ (or a natural 20) | all 3                      |

You get all 3 actions back at the end of your turn, whatever you rolled.

**Surprise.** If the heroes catch foes completely off guard, the GM may grant advantage on Initiative, or skip the roll and give everyone 3 actions. Surprised heroes might roll with disadvantage, start with 1 action, or watch the monsters go first. Being hidden or striking first isn't enough on its own.

_Core pp. 14–15._

## Turn order

- **Heroes go first**, starting with whoever is ready (or whoever makes sense), then around the table. **Monsters usually act last**, and a monster or monster group acts at the same point every round.
- A **turn** is one hero or monster group acting (about 6 seconds). A **round** is everyone taking a turn. An **encounter** is all the rounds of one fight.
- **1/turn** abilities can be used again on someone else's turn if you find a way (Sneak Attack on an Opportunity Attack). **1/round** abilities reset when your own turn begins.
- **Spreading an action over turns**: something that costs more than 1 action can be spent across turns while you hold Concentration and do nothing else except free actions.

_Core p. 15._

## Attacking

- Roll the dice on the weapon, spell or ability and deal that much damage. **If the Primary Die (the leftmost die) shows a 1, you miss.**
- **Critical hits explode**: if the Primary Die rolls its maximum, roll it again and add it, and keep going for as long as it rolls the max. **Crits ignore monster armor.**
- **Rushed attacks**: every attack after your first in a turn adds a cumulative disadvantage. Your third attack with a short sword rolls 3d6 and keeps the lowest. For abilities that call for a save, the enemy gains advantage instead.
- **Area and multi-target attacks** never miss or crit, and one roll applies to every target.
- Weapons you aren't proficient with can't crit.

_Core pp. 13, 32, 44, 56._

## Monster armor

Most monsters are unarmored. Armored ones shrug off part of your damage:

| Monster      | Damage it takes                               |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| Unarmored    | Everything: dice plus modifiers               |
| Medium armor | The dice only; positive modifiers are ignored |
| Heavy armor  | Half the dice; modifiers ignored              |

**Crits and damage vulnerabilities ignore armor.** A vulnerable unarmored creature takes double damage instead.

_Core pp. 14, 56._

## Minions

Weak foes that **die from any damage**. They move and attack together, **can't crit**, and all minions attacking one target count as a single attack, so you can Defend or Interpose against them at once. Big hits may spill over to other minions in range at the GM's call.

_Core p. 14; GM's Guide p. 24._

## Range, reach and cover

- With no range given, an attack has **Reach 1**.
- **Ranged attacks have disadvantage if any enemy is adjacent to you.** Reach attacks don't.
- **Long range**: take 1 disadvantage for +2 Range, up to +6.
- **Cover** (mostly hidden from sight) gives disadvantage to attacks against the creature. **Full cover** means it usually can't be targeted.
- **Hiding**: with cover, spend an action on a **DC 15 Stealth** check (automatic with full cover). Your first attack while hidden has advantage, and then you're revealed, unless it kills and nobody else can see you.

_Core p. 10._

## Grappling

Grab a creature within reach with at least one free arm: it makes a **STR or DEX save against 10 + your STR or DEX**. If it fails and is your size or smaller it's **Grappled**; if it's larger, you're **Riding** it. Forced movement, incapacitation, or an action and a successful STR or DEX save ends it. **Restrained** is the same but from objects (chains, rope, roots), ignores size, and ends by any sensible means.

_Core p. 10._
